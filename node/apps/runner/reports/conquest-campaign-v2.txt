Conquest executable campaigns — implementation and evaluation

Implemented
1. Frozen comparison player: tests/apps/runner/fixtures/conquest-v1/ contains the
   adapter and strategic context from the previous six-game run. Import paths
   are adjusted; a manifest records original and fixture SHA-256 digests. Both
   arms deliberately share the same game engine and provider protocol.
2. Concrete campaigns: each objective includes a staging territory, a connected
   hostile route of at most six captures, origin reserve, available strength,
   estimated required strength, placement suggestion and funding deficit. Prefer
   an affordable prefix; otherwise estimate the route to the first target.
   Friendly territories cannot be marched through as attacks. Candidate action
   effects relate deployment, attacks and movement to the selected campaign.
3. Reassessment: before optional actions, review completed objectives/routes,
   lost staging/route, exhausted loss budgets, newly exposed continent income,
   income loss and next-attack odds below 55% (or unavailable within the bounded
   calculator). Mandatory trades/occupation take
   precedence. Reviews acknowledge the current revision, persist across restart,
   and cannot repeatedly interrupt an unchanged board. Legacy plans are upgraded.
4. Tactical risk policy: attacks with full-battle capture probability below 50%
   require victory, elimination, continent completion, income denial, a first
   capture card with no available 65% alternative, or desperate survival (at most
   three territories and outnumbered two to one). This applies to single-round
   attacks too, using full-battle odds. Unknown odds remain unknown. This changes
   runner policy, not game rules, and end-attack remains available.

Paired evaluation
12 completed local rules-engine games with real Jev requests; no network games
are counted in these 12. Seeds 1401 and 1402 were each played with the candidate
in both seats. Three incremental variants were compared to the frozen player:
route only; route plus reassessment; then all features including risk policy.
Both players used the same configured Jev connection. Every report records
source hashes, initial-position hashes, completion, winner and metric definitions.
Initial positions matched, and candidate source hashes stayed unchanged across
all three experiments. Seeds reproduce starting positions, not deterministic
model replies. Subsequent RNG use diverges when chosen moves differ.

Variant       Candidate wins    Opponent wins
Route only           2                2
+ Reassessment       2                2
+ Risk policy        2                2

The winning in-game seat for each seed was the same across all variants. These
small exploratory samples do not demonstrate a win-rate improvement.

Full-policy diagnostics (candidate / frozen opponent, across four games):
- Low-odds attacks without a policy payoff: 0 / 20.
- Failed blitzes: 11 / 30; armies lost in those blitzes: 40 / 83.
- Mean interior spare armies: 5.81 / 3.66 (unweighted per-game means).
- Mean closeout turns in won games after the defined advantage: 6 / 9.
- Provider decisions, including plans and menu navigation: 786 / 788.

Interpretation: the risk rule operationally prevents its defined class of poor
bets and the candidate incurred fewer failed-blitz casualties in this sample.
That does not prove every blocked attack was strategically wrong. More troops
remained in the interior, and there was no net win advantage. Absolute idle-army
counts also depend on territory control and game length. The closeout measure
uses a material/income threshold, not a proof that a position was winning.
Route-only and reassessment-only did not independently improve win rate;
reassessment-only consumed 1,066 candidate decisions versus 828 for its opponent.

Live verification
Two independent unstaked, untimed 2-player matches on wss://saito.io/wsopen use
one full-policy candidate and one frozen player each. The candidate uses bot-1
in the first and bot-2 in the second. Live starting positions are not matched.
Completion and winner must be confirmed by both clients. The first match was
cleanly interrupted on turn 6, saved its campaign/review anchor, and resumed the
same game with the same wallets. It continued executing signed network moves.
Both live games completed: the candidate won on turns 21 and 28, with matching
confirmation from both clients. The game assigned the candidate to in-game
player 2 in both matches despite the different bot roles. These are not matched
or counterbalanced strength trials. Exact game IDs, winners and review-trigger
counts are recorded in conquest-campaign-v2-summary.json.

Validation
The full runner test command passed 173 tests. Two unrelated suites (Paths and
Twilight) could not load because node_modules/canvas/build/Release/canvas.node
is missing. This environment dependency was not modified. Campaign tests cover
frozen-source integrity, route legality and budgets, no game-state mutation,
restart/mandatory-action precedence, event triggers, risk exceptions, stale
choices, matched starting positions and no winner attribution for capped games.
Existing Conquest tests cover request limits, navigation, hidden-information
filtering and legal actions. Live games exercise the native network path.

node tests/apps/runner/conquest-campaign-benchmark.cjs --config apps/runner/config/conquest-jev-saito.json --variant full --pairs 2 --seed 1401 --max-decisions 2000 --output /tmp/conquest-v2.json

results are not evidence about Jev strength. The cap includes planning/menu calls.

conquest-v2-full.json. Consolidated results: conquest-campaign-v2-summary.json.
Public decision traces/logs: node/tmp/conquest-campaign-v2/. Isolated wallet state
is under node/runner-data/conquest-campaign-v2/ and conquest-campaign-v2-second/;
those private directories are not report artifacts. Changes are uncommitted and
preserve the checkout's other local work. No browser bundle or server deployment
was needed; native runner clients used this checkout while playing on saito.io.

Limits
Routes are bounded greedy estimates, not a global campaign optimizer; reserves
consider current adjacent stacks, not future reinforcements or coordinated enemy
attacks. The model can depart from a campaign and may still choose an unsuitable
objective. Risk exceptions can still permit losing attacks. Overall strength
remains unproven; the measured improvement is tactical selectivity, with idle
army concentration and planning overhead still needing attention.
