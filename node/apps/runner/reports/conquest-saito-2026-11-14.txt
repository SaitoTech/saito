Conquest: five review-and-improve games, then one validation game
Date: 2026-11-14
Server: wss://saito.io/wsopen
Format: two native runner wallets, Jev for both seats, quick setup, no stake or clock.
These are live signed network games, not local-engine benchmark games. Both clients
must confirm the same game and winner before a game is counted complete.

Method
Review uses selected objectives, public positions, candidate consequences and
submitted moves. It does not reveal model reasoning; explanations below are
interpretations of observable behavior. Both seats received each update. Games
have different random positions and dice, so results do not establish a win-rate
improvement or isolate each change's causal effect.

Game 1 — complete, turn 11, bot-2 won
Formation: one seat chose Asia; the other repeatedly chose North America.
Updating: Asia's controller switched to elimination after securing its bonus.
The loser retained North America even as its foothold disappeared.
Execution: the winner converted continent income into a decisive army advantage.
Change before Game 2: added an explicit enemy-continent income-denial objective,
with completion when unified enemy control breaks. This makes disruption a
first-class objective instead of requiring the model to reinterpret conquest.

Game 2 — complete, turn 20, bot-2 won
Formation: Asia and Africa were the opening objectives. Africa developed the
stronger economic base; merely offering denial did not ensure its selection.
Updating: the leader eventually selected elimination; the loser persisted with
expansion while losing economic ground.
Execution: reinforcement choices lacked direct information about attacks they
would enable. Three blitzes had capture probabilities below 40%.
Change before Game 3: reinforcement choices now preview adjacent blitz capture
probabilities after placement, connecting deployment to immediate opportunities.

Game 3 — complete, turn 17, bot-1 won
Formation: North America versus Asia. The winner later pursued Europe and Asia.
Updating: the winner changed objectives, while the losing seat repeatedly
renewed Asia as its position deteriorated.
Execution: four sub-40% blitzes remained. A menu-budget error interrupted play;
the saved game resumed after repair and both seats confirmed completion.
Change before Game 4: save armies, income and remaining targets when selecting
an objective; report changes at subsequent decisions, including next-turn renewal.
Older saves without a snapshot remain valid and report no invented progress.

Game 4 — complete, turn 24, bot-1 won
Formation: an opening European conquest created an 11-army income.
Updating: that advantage disappeared as Europe changed hands; the eventual
winner switched to elimination.
Execution: maximum-advance attacks were frequent. Large total army strength did
not guarantee a fast closeout against scattered enemy pockets.
Change before Game 5: expose defense after troop movement, including a steamroll's
one-army origin, strongest adjacent enemy capture probability, and continent
bonus at risk. Exclude neutrals and the successfully captured target. These
estimates omit future reinforcements and coordinated attacks.

Game 5 — complete, turn 47, bot-2 won
Formation: both initially selected Asia; one then repeatedly pursued Europe.
Updating: objectives stayed repetitive despite changing fortunes. The new denial
objective was selected on turn 34, but execution remained in North America.
Execution: more attacks used explicit occupation. Troop advances repeatedly
split the moving force (for example, 25 then 13 then 7), leaving large stacks
behind. The leader took many turns to convert a large army advantage. This is a
negative finding, not proof that added defensive context improved playing strength.
Change before Game 6: objective choices and active plans now show friendly steps
to their target front; occupation and fortification preview onward attack odds.
Guidance explicitly weighs onward strength against origin defense rather than
halving the advancing force by default. Distances do not authorize illegal
connected-path fortification; actual movement remains one adjacent move.

Game 6 — complete, turn 32, bot-2 won
Formation/updating: opening Europe and Asia objectives changed with the board;
the winner moved through Europe/Asia objectives before selecting elimination
on turn 30 and finishing on turn 32. Both clients confirmed the same winner.
Execution: 86 explicit occupations advanced an average of
87.1% of available troops, versus 77.6% in Game 5; automatic steamrolls
are excluded from this comparison. Maximum legal advances occurred in
56 explicit occupations. This is behavioral evidence, not a controlled
strength result. The game still included 5 blitzes below 40% capture probability.
The final code completed live play without a runner or menu-budget failure.

Runner repairs discovered during live play
- Reused wallets could report a previous game's completion while joining a new
  game. Workers now wait for the assigned game while continuing signed retries.
- Outbox cleanup could use an unrelated game's progress to discard new Arcade
  messages. Cleanup now matches the invitation/game identity.
- Nested menu sizing omitted the back-navigation option. Budget checks now
  measure the complete menu, including navigation and selection context.
These repairs were applied when encountered, then the same invitations resumed.
No stalled attempt was counted as an additional completed game.

Validation
40 focused Conquest, multi-seat and outbox tests pass after the final changes.
The broader runner suite (before the final route/onward-preview change) passed
148 tests; Paths and Twilight could not load because the local native
canvas.node binary is absent. That dependency issue was not changed.

Evidence and scope
The companion JSON records exact game IDs, winner public keys, turns, decision
counts and all selected objectives. Public decision traces and network logs are
under node/tmp/conquest-review-saito/. Private wallets/checkpoints remain in the
isolated node/runner-data/conquest-review-saito-20261114/ directory; do not publish
that directory. No wallet keys, private hands or RNG seeds are in this report.
Code changes are local and uncommitted; existing unrelated changes were preserved.
No production deployment was performed. Native runner clients used this checkout
while communicating through saito.io; browser UI behavior was not exercised.

Remaining limitations
Plans are advisory and renewed at reinforcement; progress can inform later
choices but does not force a mid-turn objective replacement. Low-odds blitzes,
repetitive objectives and slow closeouts remain observable weaknesses. One live
validation game cannot establish a statistically reliable strength improvement.
