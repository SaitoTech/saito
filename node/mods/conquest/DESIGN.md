# Conquest — Field Atlas

The visual direction is a mid-century travel atlas: warm paper, charcoal typography, loosely brushed pigment, generous editorial whitespace and small cartographic annotations. A right-hand command panel keeps every turn on the board. Territory fills encode ownership, badges encode armies, and outlines encode interaction. Shapes and player names reinforce color so play does not depend on color alone.

The board is an SVG built from the engine's independent territory geometry. CSS custom properties define paper, ink, pigment, player colors and typography; no game logic depends on the theme. Two Three.js dice groups sit in clear space beside the attacking and defending sectors, with reduced-motion and non-WebGL support. They avoid each other, sector names and army counts, using a separate strip below crowded maps when necessary. Every roll in a blitz plays before its losses are subtracted, followed by a short pause; every participant and observer sees the same sequence. Changing the visual direction requires replacing the stylesheet and optional map geometry, not changing the rules or network protocol.

Neighbouring mainland territories share vertices so a thin paper stroke creates even country gaps. Wider channels and colored coast outlines distinguish continents; map labels show each continent's reinforcement bonus, with control-progress badges in the field guide. Cards reuse territory silhouettes with separate infantry, cavalry and artillery icons. The layout reserves a bottom row for compact player boxes, with horizontal scrolling when needed, scales the board to the remaining space, and lets turn details scroll above fixed notice and action areas, with the card selector anchored at the bottom. The enlarged map supports dragging without triggering territory actions. Troop sliders begin at the maximum legal count for quick deployment and movement.

Interaction: choose an origin, then an adjacent target. Phase-aware controls show the next legal action, army counts, independent Blitz and Steamroll options, clickable cards that automatically trade a valid selected set of three, and a clear Finish attacking button. Moving armies or skipping movement advances the turn immediately. An impossible attack or movement phase displays its reason for two seconds, then advances automatically; the pause waits for battle playback and pending card work. All viewers see the notice, while only the active player sends the phase-ending move. Controls use the active player’s colour. Setup and reinforcements support one-click placement. Conquests make the required troop move explicit unless Steamroll is enabled to advance all but one army automatically; fortification supports adjacent friendly territories. Neutral armies have a distinct hatched treatment in two-player games. A player roster with wallet identicons, a YOU badge in the top right and public card counts and reinforcement income, a dispatch journal that closes on outside clicks and rules sheet explain the current state without obscuring the board. Campaign shares the existing Saito menu row; Field guide is in the Game menu. The title sits at the bottom right beside the player boxes. The native game menu, wallet drawer and dispatch log share the atlas paper, ink, typography and subtle borders, with active-player accents; these styles apply only while Conquest is mounted. A nearby Go button at attack, movement and occupation targets uses the same settings as the main controls.

The standalone page is a local hotseat demonstration using exactly the same engine and UI as the Saito module. The module supplies a controller with `getState()`, `dispatch(action)`, `getPlayer()`, and `getPlayerName(id)`; it remains responsible for Saito turn queues and deterministic randomness. `getPlayer() === 0` enables hotseat input in the demonstration only.

Readability: board text is restored to its original sizes, including the 12px screen minimum for scaled labels. Elsewhere, the enlarged text is reduced by 33%, with a 12px minimum. Player boxes have compact spacing and smaller type. Narrow and short screens scroll rather than shrinking controls. Card faces have larger territory art, names and unit symbols. The redundant identity header is removed. Stats opens a themed dialog with per-player dice histograms and casualties, filtered by all campaigns or the campaign number shown in the header. Every attack has its own losses/defeated dispatch. Statistics are recorded by the deterministic rules engine, never by animation or rendering.
