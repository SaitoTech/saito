RED IMPERIUM — SECTOR ART UPGRADE PROMPT

TASK

Upgrade the supplied Red Imperium sector/hex artwork into a polished, professional science-fiction board-game illustration.

Use the supplied image as the AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE for the composition, geometry, planet placement, and asset boundaries.

The goal is to substantially improve the visual quality while preserving ALL gameplay-relevant geometry and layout.

The result should look like a professionally illustrated space strategy board-game tile: restrained, coherent, atmospheric, readable, colorful, and designed to have ships, units, markers, tokens, labels, and other game elements layered over it.

THIS IS GAME BOARD ART, NOT CINEMATIC CONCEPT ART.

The artwork must prioritize gameplay visibility, compositional consistency, and tile compatibility over drama, spectacle, photographic realism, or cinematic lighting.

DO NOT redesign the composition.

DO NOT reinterpret the geometry.

DO NOT create a new hex.

DO NOT change the proportions of the supplied hex.

DO NOT change the location or size of any planet.

Only improve the visual rendering of the existing composition.

Their geometry is locked.


==================================================
4. PLANET PRESERVATION AND IMPROVEMENT
==================================================

Preserve every existing planet as the same planet occupying exactly the same location and physical size.

Improve the planets so that they appear like convincing astronomical bodies rather than flat graphical spheres.

Planets should have:

- believable surface texture
- natural geological or atmospheric variation
- distinctive coloration
- coherent surface detail
- subtle atmospheric effects where appropriate
- gentle spherical depth
- restrained illumination
- visually interesting but believable surface features

The planets are the PRIMARY VISUAL OBJECTS in the sector.

They should provide most of the visual character of the tile.

Planet colors and surface textures may be vivid and distinctive.

The planets may be much more colorful and detailed than the background.

Do not make the planets:

- excessively glossy
- metallic
- magical
- fantastical
- artificially luminous
- overly polished
- excessively CGI-like

Avoid exaggerated 3D rendering.

Avoid cinematic movie-poster aesthetics.

These are board-game planets, not movie props.


==================================================
5. PLANET LIGHTING — EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
==================================================

Use SOFT, EVEN, RESTRAINED lighting.

The purpose of lighting is to establish the spherical form of the planet WITHOUT making large portions of the planet disappear into darkness.

The entire visible planetary surface should remain readable.

The player should be able to clearly inspect the planet.

DO NOT create:

- a dark underside
- a black hemisphere
- a large dark crescent
- a strong day/night boundary
- a strong terminator line
- extreme light-versus-shadow contrast
- dramatic backlighting
- strong cinematic rim lighting
- a large area of completely obscured surface
- a cinematic silhouette

DO NOT allow 30%, 40%, 50%, or more of a planet to disappear into shadow.

Ideally, almost the entire visible planetary disk should remain readable.

Any shadowing must be VERY SUBTLE.

A gentle gradient across the surface is acceptable.

A small amount of shading near one edge is acceptable.

But the darkest portion of the planet must still retain substantial visible surface color, texture, and detail.

The planet should read primarily through:

- surface texture
- color variation
- geological detail
- atmospheric detail
- restrained shading
- subtle highlights

rather than dramatic light/shadow contrast.

Think:

"beautifully illustrated planetary surface with gentle spherical depth"

NOT:

"cinematic 3D-rendered planet illuminated by a distant sun."

GAMEPLAY VISIBILITY IS MORE IMPORTANT THAN PHYSICAL LIGHTING REALISM.

Do not introduce dramatic directional sunlight merely to make the planet appear three-dimensional.

The planet does not need to visibly correspond to a particular unseen sun.


==================================================
6. SPACE BACKGROUND — CORE VISUAL PHILOSOPHY
==================================================

The background should establish that the planet exists within a large galaxy.

It is NOT the primary artwork.

The planets are the artwork.

SPACE IS THE ENVIRONMENT.

EMPTY SPACE IS INTENTIONAL.

Do not attempt to fill empty areas.

Do not add visual details simply because a region of the tile appears empty.

Quiet areas of space are desirable.

Large areas of relatively featureless dark space are desirable.

The background should feel:

- deep
- spacious
- atmospheric
- restrained
- believable
- sophisticated
- compatible with gameplay

It should NOT feel:

- busy
- noisy
- spectacular
- cinematic
- poster-like
- overloaded
- artificially decorated
- designed to show off the background


==================================================
7. DEFAULT BACKGROUND COLOR
==================================================

Use a DEEP BLUE-BLACK space background.

The blackness of the galaxy should subtly VEER BLUE.

Prefer:

- deep navy
- blue-black
- very dark blue-gray
- extremely dark desaturated blue

Avoid pure flat black as the dominant background.

The blue coloration should be subtle.

Do NOT turn the background into bright blue space.

The overall background should remain dark enough for planets, ships, units, and markers to remain immediately readable.

The background should feel like deep space with a slight cool atmospheric character.

A restrained blue-black foundation should be the common visual language connecting the sector collection.


==================================================
8. BACKGROUND COLOR VARIATION
==================================================

Individual sectors may have subtle atmospheric coloration.

Acceptable background color influences include:

- deep red
- muted crimson
- dark purple
- subdued teal
- blue
- blue-gray
- restrained amber
- other natural astronomical colors

Color variation is desirable because different sectors should have distinctive identities.

However, background color must remain SUBORDINATE to the planet.

A sector may have a recognizable red atmospheric character.

Another may be predominantly blue.

Another may contain faint purple or teal coloration.

Another may be almost entirely blue-black.

The collection should feel like different regions of the same galaxy.

Do NOT make every sector use the same background coloration.

Do NOT make every sector predominantly red.

Do NOT make every sector predominantly purple.

Do NOT make every sector predominantly blue.

The shared identity should come from the restrained visual language, not from identical background colors.


==================================================
9. NEBULAE, DUST, AND ASTRONOMICAL STRUCTURES
==================================================

Nebulae and diffuse astronomical structures are OPTIONAL.

They are NOT required.

A sector may have:

- a faint diffuse nebula
- subtle interstellar dust
- a soft colored haze
- a broad cloud structure
- faint astronomical background texture

OR it may have almost nothing beyond the starfield.

Both are desirable possibilities.

When astronomical structures are used, they should generally be:

- broad
- soft
- diffuse
- atmospheric
- low to moderate contrast
- naturally irregular
- integrated into the background

They should provide ATMOSPHERE, not SPECTACLE.

A colorful red background is acceptable.

A dramatic red nebula filling the entire tile is usually NOT acceptable.

A beautiful diffuse nebula is acceptable when its intensity remains controlled.

--------------------------------------------------
DO NOT USE BACKGROUND STRUCTURES TO FILL SPACE
--------------------------------------------------

Do NOT see an empty area and place a nebula there merely because it looks unfinished.

Do NOT add a cloud simply to balance the composition.

Do NOT add a bright feature simply because the opposite side contains a planet.

Do NOT fill empty quadrants.

Do NOT artificially balance the background.

Uneven visual density is natural and desirable.

--------------------------------------------------
DO NOT FRAME THE PLANETS
--------------------------------------------------

Do not create:

- clouds wrapping around planets
- luminous arcs surrounding planets
- glowing bands tracing planets
- nebulae forming halos around planets
- clouds deliberately placed behind planets as a frame
- luminous structures pointing toward planets
- visual pathways leading toward planets
- decorative structures connecting planets

The planets should appear to EXIST WITHIN the space background.

The background should not appear to have been constructed specifically around them.

--------------------------------------------------
NO REPEATED BACKGROUND MOTIFS
--------------------------------------------------

Across different Red Imperium sectors, do NOT repeatedly use:

- the same diagonal nebula
- the same horizontal cloud
- the same vertical cloud
- the same curved band
- the same red cloud formation
- the same blue wisp
- the same glowing streak
- the same planetary framing structure
- the same background orientation

Different sectors must have different background compositions.

The collection should feel naturally varied rather than generated from one decorative template.


==================================================
10. STARFIELD
==================================================

The starfield must be SUBTLE.

Stars exist primarily to communicate depth and establish that the planet is in space.

They are NOT visual subjects.

Most stars should be:

- tiny
- faint
- low contrast
- relatively cool or neutral in color
- visually subordinate

Use enough stars to establish a convincing space environment, but do not make the starfield busy.

The viewer should perceive the stars primarily as background texture.

--------------------------------------------------
BRIGHT STARS
--------------------------------------------------

Bright stars should be RARE.

Very bright stars should be EXTREMELY RARE.

Do NOT create numerous large stars.

Do NOT create numerous bright stars.

Do NOT create clusters of conspicuous stars.

Do NOT create stars that repeatedly pull the viewer's eye away from the planet.

Avoid:

- giant white stars
- giant colored stars
- strong starbursts
- diffraction spikes
- lens flares
- pronounced stellar halos
- repeated glowing stars
- cinematic stellar effects

A small number of somewhat brighter stars is acceptable for natural variation.

But they should remain exceptions.

--------------------------------------------------
STAR COLOR
--------------------------------------------------

Most stars should be:

- near-white
- slightly cool white
- slightly warm white

Do not deliberately populate the image with bright blue, yellow, orange, and red stars as decorative objects.

Subtle natural color variation is acceptable.

Strong star coloration is not a visual objective.

--------------------------------------------------
STARFIELD HIERARCHY
--------------------------------------------------

The viewer's attention should naturally go:

1. PLANET
2. BACKGROUND ATMOSPHERE
3. STARFIELD

If a viewer is looking at the planet, the stars behind it should NOT cause the eye to jump away from the planet.

The starfield should establish depth without demanding attention.


==================================================
11. BACKGROUND VISUAL ENERGY
==================================================

The background should have LOWER visual energy than the planet.

This means:

- lower contrast
- lower saturation
- lower brightness
- less sharp detail
- fewer prominent focal points

The planet should remain the dominant visual object.

Do not compensate for a bright background by putting the planet into shadow.

If the background is colorful, the planet must still remain clearly visible.

If the background is brighter in one region, do not darken the planet dramatically to compensate.

The planet must remain readable regardless of background treatment.


==================================================
12. TILE EDGE TREATMENT
==================================================

The sector will be placed beside other Red Imperium sector tiles.

The background must therefore work as a TILE, not merely as an isolated illustration.

The outer regions of the hex should generally become QUIETER and DARKER toward the perimeter.

Background visual energy should generally diminish toward the edges.

Prefer a natural transition toward deep blue-black space around the outer portions of the hex.

Avoid placing major high-contrast features directly against the perimeter.

Avoid placing:

- giant bright stars
- luminous nebula edges
- bright clouds
- glowing bands
- dramatic structures

directly on the outer boundary whenever possible.

Important background structures should generally be concentrated toward the interior.

A dramatic central or interior nebula is acceptable if it naturally fades toward the perimeter.

The edge should NOT be a hard artificial black vignette.

Instead, use a natural falloff of visual activity.

The goal is that this sector can be placed beside a completely different sector without producing a visually jarring collision between two unrelated scenes.

The outer edge should feel like it is disappearing into the same deep galaxy that surrounds the neighboring tile.


==================================================
13. COMPOSITION
==================================================

Do not force symmetry.

Do not force balance.

Do not create visual counterweights merely to make the image look "complete."

Do not fill empty quadrants.

Do not place decorative background elements opposite planets simply to balance the composition.

Natural asymmetry is desirable.

The composition should feel discovered rather than designed.

The planets should feel as though they naturally occupy their fixed positions within a larger astronomical environment.

These sector tiles form a large standardized collection.

They will be rearranged into different board configurations from game to game.

The tiles therefore need to feel like parts of the SAME GALAXY.

- planet rendering quality

- readable planetary surfaces
- deep blue-black space
- subtle starfields
- restrained background atmosphere
- natural astronomical coloration
- subdued tile edges
- gameplay-oriented contrast

Consistency does NOT mean identical compositions.

Do not make every tile look like the same background with a different planet inserted.

Each sector should have its own visual identity while remaining compatible with the collection.

The final image will have:

- ships
- units
- fighters
- markers
- tokens
- labels
- counters
- other interactive elements

PRIORITIZE READABILITY OVER SPECTACLE.

Avoid giant bright stars behind gameplay objects.

Avoid strong glowing regions that compete with units.

Avoid excessive background detail.

The planet must remain clearly visible.

Game pieces must remain clearly visible.

The background must recede.
