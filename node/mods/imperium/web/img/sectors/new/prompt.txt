RED IMPERIUM — SECTOR ART UPGRADE PROMPT

TASK
Upgrade the supplied Red Imperium sector/hex artwork into a polished, professional science-fiction board-game illustration.

Use the supplied image as the authoritative source for the composition.

The goal is to substantially improve the visual quality while preserving all gameplay-relevant geometry and layout.

The result should look like a professionally illustrated space strategy board-game tile: restrained, coherent, atmospheric, readable, and designed to have ships, units, markers, tokens, and other game elements layered over it.

This is GAME BOARD ART, not cinematic concept art.

The artwork must prioritize gameplay visibility and visual consistency over drama, spectacle, contrast, or photographic realism.

Do NOT redesign the composition.

==================================================
1. HARD GEOMETRY REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

These requirements are mandatory and take precedence over artistic changes.

PRESERVE THE ORIGINAL CANVAS EXACTLY:

908 × 800 pixels.

Preserve the exact existing hexagonal silhouette.

Preserve the exact dimensions, orientation, proportions, and position of the hex within the 908 × 800 canvas.

The transparent regions outside the hex must remain transparent.

Do not crop, expand, rotate, skew, or otherwise alter the canvas or hex geometry.

PRESERVE PLANET GEOMETRY EXACTLY.

For every planet in the supplied image:

- Preserve its exact center position.
- Preserve its exact diameter/size.
- Preserve its circular silhouette.
- Preserve its relative position to every other planet.
- Do not move the planet.
- Do not resize the planet.
- Do not rotate or reposition the planet.
- Do not merge planets.
- Do not split planets.
- Do not change the number of planets.

Planet geometry is a GAMEPLAY AND LAYOUT CONSTRAINT, not an artistic suggestion.

If explicit planet coordinates and dimensions are provided below, those values are authoritative.

The AI must not reinterpret or rebalance the composition.

Only the visual appearance of the existing objects should change.

==================================================
2. PLANET PRESERVATION AND IMPROVEMENT
==================================================

Preserve every existing planet as the same planet occupying exactly the same location and physical size.

Improve the planets so that they appear like convincing astronomical bodies rather than flat graphical spheres.

Planets should have:

- believable surface texture
- natural geological or atmospheric variation
- subtle atmospheric effects where appropriate
- coherent illumination
- restrained depth
- subtle curvature
- visually interesting but believable surface features

The planets must remain highly visible against the background.

Do not make the planets excessively glossy, luminous, metallic, magical, or fantastical.

Avoid exaggerated CGI rendering.

Avoid dramatic cinematic lighting.

Avoid strong 3D effects.

These are board-game planets, not movie props.

--------------------------------------------------
PLANET LIGHTING — VERY IMPORTANT
--------------------------------------------------

Use SOFT, EVEN, RESTRAINED lighting.

The purpose of lighting is to establish the spherical form of the planet without making large portions of the planet disappear into darkness.

The planets should remain readable across almost their entire visible surface.

DO NOT create a dark underside.

DO NOT create a black hemisphere.

DO NOT create a large dark crescent.

DO NOT create a strong terminator line.

DO NOT create extreme light-versus-shadow contrast.

DO NOT use dramatic backlighting.

DO NOT use strong cinematic rim lighting.

DO NOT make 30%, 40%, or 50% of a planet disappear into shadow.

Any shadowing must be SUBTLE.

The darkest portion of the planet should still retain substantial visible surface color and detail.

The planet should read primarily through:

- surface texture
- color variation
- atmospheric detail
- restrained shading
- subtle highlights

rather than through dramatic light/shadow contrast.

Think:

"illustrated planetary surface with gentle depth"

NOT:

"cinematic 3D-rendered sphere."

GAMEPLAY VISIBILITY IS MORE IMPORTANT THAN PHYSICAL LIGHTING REALISM.

==================================================
3. DEFAULT SPACE BACKGROUND
==================================================

IMPORTANT:

The DEFAULT Red Imperium space background contains NO NEBULA.

Do not add nebulae, gas clouds, cosmic clouds, luminous red clouds, colorful dust formations, glowing space banners, or dramatic interstellar structures unless they are explicitly requested in the per-image instructions.

The normal background should be:

- deep blue-black
- dark navy
- very subtle blue-gray variation
- sparse stars
- extremely subtle astronomical dust if appropriate
- gentle tonal variation

The default visual language is a QUIET STARFIELD.

The space should feel deep and vast without containing spectacular astronomical objects.

The background should be intentionally boring compared with the planets.

--------------------------------------------------
DEFAULT BACKGROUND — ABSOLUTE RESTRICTIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Unless specifically requested otherwise, DO NOT include:

- red nebulae

- blue nebulae

DO NOT interpret "space" as "nebula."

The default Red Imperium background is simply:

DARK SPACE + SUBTLE STARFIELD.

If a nebula is not explicitly requested, there must be NO NEBULA.

==================================================
4. STARS
==================================================

Stars should be subtle and subordinate to the planets.

Use:

- small stars
- sparse to moderate density
- mostly dim stars
- subtle variation in brightness
- occasional slightly brighter stars

Avoid:

- large glowing stars
- starbursts
- lens flares
- giant white stars
- clusters of extremely bright stars
- dramatic stellar effects

The stars should establish that the planets are in space without becoming an important visual feature themselves.

A player should be able to place a ship directly over the starfield and still see the ship clearly.

==================================================
5. STANDARDIZED RED IMPERIUM VISUAL LANGUAGE
==================================================

These sector tiles form a large standardized collection.

They will be rearranged into different board configurations from game to game.

The individual tiles therefore MUST NOT compete for attention.

The desired visual hierarchy is:

1. GAME PIECES
2. PLANETS
3. SUBTLE SPACE BACKGROUND

The background should NEVER compete with gameplay pieces.

The planets should provide most of the visual character of each tile.

Different tiles may have different planet colors, textures, and types.

The background should remain broadly consistent between tiles.

The tiles should look like parts of the same game.

7. VISUAL DETAIL
==================================================

Acceptable background detail includes:

However, background detail must remain low contrast.

Do not use large visual structures to create interest.

Do not use nebulae to create interest.

Do not use dramatic lighting to create interest.

The PLANETS should provide the visual interest.

Think:

"quiet and sophisticated"

rather than:

"epic and spectacular."

8. GAMEPLAY READABILITY
==================================================

This is BOARD-GAME ART.

The final image will have ships, units, markers, tokens, labels, and other interactive elements layered over the sector.

Therefore:

PRIORITIZE READABILITY OVER SPECTACLE.

The background must be dark enough and quiet enough that game pieces remain immediately visible.

Maintain relatively quiet areas around and between planets.

Avoid high-contrast background elements behind likely gameplay locations.

Avoid bright regions behind planets if they make the planet difficult to distinguish.

Avoid strong background highlights.

Avoid strong color contrast in the background.

The planets should be clearly visible.

Game pieces should be clearly visible.

The background should recede.
